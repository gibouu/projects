Thumb

Your phone, on autopilot. Thumb taps through your web tasks while you sit on the train, learns each site, and makes the call when a website can't help.

The idea

The first time is slow and costs a model. Every time after should be nearly free.

Thumb runs websites inside its own browser, in your logged-in sessions, and works through them one action at a time. It reads the page directly, its visible text and every button, link and field, instead of guessing from screenshots. Because it owns the browser it sees every action and its result, and that trace is what it learns from: a run that ends on a real receipt becomes the route for that site, and the next run replays it.

What it does

  1. Flat huntingReads every listing site, removes duplicates, scores each flat against your profile and applies to the ones you approve.
  2. Job applicationsSays in one line whether a job fits, then fills the whole multi-step application.
  3. The weekly websiteWatches for the moment booking opens on an old club website and books a place.
  4. Teach ThumbDo a task once in Thumb's browser. It records the steps, never passwords, and turns them into a tile it replays.
  5. Call the landlordAn ElevenLabs voice agent says it is your AI assistant, asks your questions and files the answers.
  6. Self-repairWhen a site changes, the model takes over only the changed steps, and the route is rewritten.

Architecture

One action per step (click, type, select, navigate, scroll, extract, done or fail), chosen by a forced function call. Anything that sends something waits for your approval, and an application only counts when the page shows a receipt.

Data on Aiven

PostgreSQL, the memory

ClickHouse and Valkey

  • Valkey streams new listings and openings: the phone reacts within about 2 seconds
